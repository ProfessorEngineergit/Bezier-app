Privacy.

The short version: Bezier does not measure what you do, and sends nothing on its own.

The app

  • Your projects stay on your computer. They are kept in the app's own storage on this device.
  • No telemetry, no tracking, no account. Bezier does not count downloads or starts.
  • One request by itself, in the desktop app only: when it opens, it asks api.github.com whether a newer release is out. GitHub sees your address and the name of the app, nothing about you or your projects. It is a switch in Settings, Updates (Look at start). Bezier never installs anything by itself; the download is a link to the release page. The browser version makes no such request.
  • Off until you switch them on: Community, Collaboration and the plugins and wiki of the alpha channel talk to a server, and only to the server whose address you enter. Assistants over MCP connect to a program on your own computer.
  • Report a problem opens a GitHub issue form in your browser with the report text filled in. The text is in the address of that page, so GitHub receives it when the page opens. You decide whether to submit it there. The picture, if you add one, goes to your clipboard and not to GitHub.
  • Links open in your browser only when you click them, and only https://github.com/ addresses are opened from the desktop app.

The Windows installer comes with an uninstaller.

This website

This site sets no cookies, has no analytics and loads nothing from other companies. Its fonts and pictures come from this server.

The host, IONOS SE, keeps the usual server logs (address, time, page, browser) for as long as its own terms say, and uses them for security and to keep the site running. We do not read them for anything else and do not join them with other data. The basis is our interest in a working, secure site (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Your rights

You can ask us what we hold about you, to correct or delete it, to restrict its use, or to hand it over (Art. 15 to 18 and 20 GDPR), and you can object to its use (Art. 21). We hold next to nothing, so most of that is quickly answered. You may also complain to the data protection authority of your state (Art. 77 GDPR).
